### B2. What bookers need (distilled, with what holds for Gabe's rooms)

1. **A hosted page, not a PDF**, that opens fast on a phone with the strongest material first; a one-page PDF only as a backup.
2. **One live clip of a real show**, ideally a whole or nearly whole song with a visible crowd. Clean audio and steady picture beat budget. Gabe has this: Scenic Route live at Crooked Eye (3:20).
3. **Proof of draw and track record**: past show dates and rooms, honest numbers, repeat invitations. Guides warn that exaggeration hurts more than modest numbers. For Gabe's rooms (open mics, bars, a church) the honest proof is a long list of rooms he keeps coming back to.
4. **Photos**: two or three current shots, horizontal and vertical, about 1500 px on the long edge or more, a clean download, photographer credit. Gabe has exactly one hi-res photo (Alexander Kealey, 6000x4000) and two small video stills.
5. **Bio in two lengths** (one line for listings, a short paragraph) and links to listen. He now has a good short bio on Spotify (see A2).
6. **Stage plot and one-page tech rider with an input list** (solo act: 2 to 3 channels; four-piece: 10 to 14): answers the sound engineer's questions before they ask. This is the thing a venue sound person actually needs; we know nothing of Gabe's setup yet.
7. **A named contact and a clear next step**, ideally a pre-filled subject line and a reply-time promise. Separate booking and press addresses only matter for bigger acts.
8. **Press quotes**: useful, but real ones only; none of the small-act pages above had any. Do not invent.
9. **Freshness**: update about every 90 days; stale photos or dates hurt. This is hard rule 3 again.
10. **For faith bookers** (churches, youth events, festivals): they ask whether the set is clean/all-ages and whether he'll share a short message; none of the guides cover it and neither did the artist sites. It is an open question for Gabe, but a simple line ("faith-based, honest lyrics") answers part of it.
